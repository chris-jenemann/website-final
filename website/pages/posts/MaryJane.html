Run

Mary Jane

Moab, UT 3/17

Chris Jenemann 6/8/26

Quick jog through the desert

Fox and I took an afternoon of our week in Moab to go explore one of the local slot canyons known as Mary Jane Canyon or Professor creek. After moutain biking in the morning we departed from our camp and drove east on SR-128 out to where the trail head was. The drive out was beautiful, with striking red canyon walls along side the road and the emrald green Colorado River below us. We reached the trail head which was framed by Castelon Tower and Fisher Mesa and backdroped by the snowcapped La Sals.

We treated this excursion as a relaxed trail run, jogging and and stopping whenever we felt fit, though never wasting too much time at any one stop. As we made our way father along the 4 mile trail the canyon walls slowly grew around us, starting as small sandstone bluffs.

By the time we were fully in the canyon the wall towered over 50 feel overhead at points showing the power erosion has over thousands of years. Within the cave pockets birds had created nests and filled the canyon with echoing chirps sounding over the distorted babble of the creek.

We ran and hopped out way up the creek trying to stay dry before the the creek engulfed the entire canyon floor. At this point we dicted our shoes and waded the last stretch up to the waterfall and chockstone that signal the end of the passable canyon.

Heading back out we could fully take in the impact of the water and shade in the dessert as it was almost uncomfortably chilly near the end of the canyon, whereas it was scorching on the desert surface. The glowing red rocks combined with mist from the spring creek cast a hazy glow over the environment creating a soft and etheral enviorment.

After emerging from the canyon walls we were once again treated to the view of the mesas and towers of southern Utah. Near by were the iconic climbing areas of Castelton and Fisher Towers. Though we didn’t make time get on any real rock I hope to one day give a shot at Castelton as its one of the most athestic dessert lines in the Country.

We slogged the 4 mile back to the car only stopping briefly to dip our hats in the stream and eat a waffle. Our week northern bodies were yet to become acoustom the the sun and heat that comes in the desert even in spring. Though the jog remained slow as we enjoyed the scenery and bounced from one side of the river to the other.

Once back at camp we enjoyed our last night in Utah by eating the last of our Carmel apples, the staple dessert of the trip. Mourning the end of such a fun trip we stayed up late under the starts joking and enjoying the last of our Campground along the Colorado.
