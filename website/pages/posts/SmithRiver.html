The Smith River, MT 5/23

Chris Jenemann 6/6/26

5 Days on Montana's most scenic river

My girlfriend, Margaret, had been putting in a permit for Montana’s Smith River ever since she was 12. This spring, she finally pulled one after 7 long years of applying and over two decades since her parents had pulled one. I was lucky enough to be invited along on the float, along with a group of Margaret’s dad’s friends’ friends, a situation that is too long and convoluted to explain concisely. Regardless, we had a harrowing journey at times, but with lots of great memories to go along with it.

After making the, by Montana standards, short drive to White Sulfur Springs and Camp Baker, we packed our boats and pushed off. The Smith River is a 60-mile stretch of river that flows through central Montana. The river is characterized by sheer canyon walls, and being one of the few multiday river trips in Montana. Its beauty and length make it the only river in the state to require a permit to float, making Margaret's winning of the permit even more special. We floated a short 6 miles to Indian Spring Campground, located by Indian Spring, where we filled up our water before launching the next day. I actually caught my first two fish on a fly rod on this section of the river, with the second even being on a dry! Overall, this day was pretty good, though there was a definite feeling of stress in the air that came with loading and unloading the boats for the first time. Our companions for this trip were older, so they needed a hand with a lot of things, putting some more stress on Margaret's family and me.

Waking up at Indian Springs was cold. The day was long as well, with 18 miles ahead of us before our next camp at Sunset Cliffs. At this point, it was becoming clearer that the other half of our group needed more help with camp stuff, which often fell on us. This is not to denigrate our companions, as they were great people who really did seem to be grateful to be there, though, due to a combination of old age and a lack of outdoor experience, did slow the group down as a whole. Regardless, we got on the river and tried to make up time after a slow start to the morning. The day was perfect with a bright, clear sky and little wind, so we made decent time. I was behind the sticks for the first half of the day with Margaret in a small AIRE Puma(seen in the thumbnail image), along with all our gear. This little raft was sweet and a great fishing boat, though too undersized to be at home on a multiday river trip. We got slightly separated from the rest of Margaret’s family, and along with that came a lack of snacks, making me very hungry. After chasing down the green boat, we stopped for lunch and switched out with the parents on the Puma and Cormac, Margaret's brother, Margaret, and me on the Green AIRE.

After switching, disaster struck in the form of a flipped Puma. Going through a tight section of the canyon, Margaret’s mom, Jennifer, came into a turn with too much speed and a raft over-loaded with 5 days of gear. This resulted in the raft being high-sided alongside the canyon wall, where two currents pinned the boat and Jonathan and Jennifer. This was terrifying to watch, though to his credit, after watching his parents go through a near-death experience, Cormac stayed focused and navigated the turn with no issue. By the time we made it through the parents and freed both themselves and the rafts, escaping unharmed though properly soaked and rightfully scared. After regrouping and taking a breather, I took the Puma boat back, and we set off down the river, with still around 11 miles to go. The rest of the day went uneventfully, and we got to camp grateful that the only real loss of the day was Margaret’s fly rod.

Camp at Sunset Cliffs was amazing, and we were treated to some delicious pasta out of the Dutch oven. After rowing close to 15 miles, Cormac and I ducked out of setup duty to go fish, though we were unsuccessful. We finished the night early, and I crawled into Margaret’s and my tent, grateful for my plush sleeping pad. Quick aside, the picture below was taken at camp that night, and we are all wearing the same Patagonia shorts, though mine have been worn down closer to a purple at this point. Baggies are the best, these have been going for 5 years now!

The next day started with another disaster. One of the people coming along from the trip had suffered from an impacted inner ear that was once again flaring up. There was talk of a Heli-vac, though it was decided in the end that we would instead push 5 or so miles to Heaven on Earth Ranch, where they could be picked up. After the short and uneventful float down to the ranch, we stopped and dropped off two members of our group and got some expensive but delicious ice cream sandwiches.

Now down from our original 9 to just 7, we finished off the day without incident events other than Cormac taking a break from rowing and landing his first fish of the trip, a small rainbow on the dry. Fishing was decent that day, though I spent most of it rowing, so I didn’t catch any. I believe this was the day that Jennifer caught an impressive brown, though it may have been later in the trip. One of our group members, a seasoned fisherman, was able to catch quite a few on salmon flies. That night we ate the dinner that the extracted group left for us, along with a liter of Coke I was able to snag from the pair. We ate the most delicious Mediterranean chicken with dates and couscous. The Coke was also a nice treat. Parker Flats, where we camped, was as beautiful as the rest of the sites we stayed at.

The penultimate day was a short one with only six miles, though a forecasted rain storm kept us moving fast down the river. Though the storm would end up passing over us, leaving only a few drops. Getting to camp early in the day was a much-needed relief from being on the river. We stayed at Ridge Top, where the fishing was great. I caught 2 or 3 decent rainbows all on the dry, and Margaret caught her first fish ever, a beautiful rainbow trout.

After the clouds cleared, the sun came out, so we spent some time wading around and exploring the river around the camp. I found this little jumping spider and went to test out my new macro lens, which I picked up in a poor FB Marketplace financial decision.

Though I think it may have been worth it after getting some sweet shots of this rainbow. I’ll have to use it some more on the colored up cutthroat I catch out of the alpine.

Can you really ask for a better first fish? A rainbow out of Montana’s most beautiful river. Though her rod broke in the boat flip, she made do with mine and caught this beautiful fish.

Though her handling skills may need some work.

That night, we were treated to some of the best steak I’ve had. We ate thick-cut steaks from the locally famous Deemo’s butcher in Ennis, charred over the fire with fajita vegetables. I can’t put into words how good this meal was; the steak was truly out of this world.

The next morning, we packed up camp and made our way out of the winding canyon of the Smith and into the AG land of the Missouri Valley near Ulm, Montana, where we would take out. Though the scenery was especially beautiful, especially in the last few miles of the canyon, I realized how little I had picked up my camera in the last few days. Possibly a sign that I was truly appreciating the moment.

After seeing some cute sheep and an Eagle watching over the river, we wrapped up the trip at Eden Bridge, the take-out point for the Smith. The packing up was a bit of a challenge, being short a trailer, though we made it work. We stopped at a gas station to enjoy the wonders of an actual toilet and to grab some snacks for the drive home.

This trip was definitely more than I was expecting. I am somewhat serious about outdoor recreation, so dealing with the number of things going wrong was definitely a challenge. Rarely do I have an incident-free trip, along with though those incidents are usually minor ripples, not life-threatening rapids to use a river analogy. Watching my girlfriend's parents almost die, having strangers around who can’t set up their own tents, those same strangers having to get rescued halfway through our remote floating trip, and so on. Those experiences were really stressful, and it was difficult to feel in control of the situation, along with the fact that everyone else on the river seemed to know we were the biggest river chuds of them all. Regardless of these doubts, I really did enjoy myself. I think a younger, less confident me would have struggled, but I enjoyed every moment of the trip. Being away from technology and dealing with adversity, I think, is one of the keys to happiness, and this trip provided both. At the end, I spent 5 days in a beautiful place with great people, and I would absolutely do it again. Overall, I’m incredibly grateful to have been invited and have been able to go and spend time on the river, especially with my wonderful girlfriend.
