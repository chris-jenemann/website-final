Highway 2

Nyack, MT 2/26

Chris Jenemann 6/5/26

Trees and Despair with my Friends

After a fun ski trip to Glacier the previous weekend, I was eager to get back out in the skin track. My buddy Henry Sund invited Elliot Stimpson and me to his family's cabin in Whitefish for the weekend. Any chance of finding good snow was out of the question; it had not snowed for weeks, and National Weather was calling for highs near 50 degrees. We chose to check out a classic BC zone in the mountains surrounding Nyack, Montana, hoping to at least score a scenic tour and scout for lines in the future. We drove the 30 or so minutes up Highway 2 to the pullout near the trailhead. Already, the clear skies revealed the beautiful peaks of Glacier across the Flathead River.

We booted up a forest service road a few hundred vertical feet before finding decent enough cover to throw the skins on. As we ascended, the temperature rose with us, and by the time we poked out of the forest and onto the ridge, we had all abandoned our layers. Climate disaster aside, the views were enchanting. Glacier’s alpine faces glowed in the sun, showing off their glacial horns and carved ridges adorned in rime.

The Great Bear Wilderness was no slouch either, with Nyack Mountain looking like something out of a TGR Film.

We continued up the ridge, soaking in the sun and views. The already slushy snow was rapidly heating, clumping to our skins and making climbing a slog. At this point, we were still following an old skin track up the ridge, though being careful not to expose ourselves to the rapidly heating snow. Mt. Saint Nicholas, behind us, kept grabbing my attention, looking more at place in the Alps than the northern Rockies with its distinctive glacial horn.

Nyack was also looking more and more dramatic as we gained elevation. Those spines reminded me of the steep faces of Alaska's coast I saw last year while on a trip to Alyeska.

At this point, all avalanche terrain was out of the question with how warm it was, so we were at a decision point. Do we follow the ridge all the way around to the other side of the drainage and drop down to the other side, as seen in the picture below, or drop down into the drainage below us and walk out there?

In the end, we decided to drop down into the drainage directly below us to save some distance. In hindsight, this was a massive mistake, though it made sense at the time. With short days and unfamiliar terrain, it seemed like the right call to ski the low-angle terrain and follow the valley back to the highway.

We dropped down off the ridge and found pretty decent slush skiing. Riding slush in February is not necessarily at the top of my ski goals, but we had fun regardless. Once at the base of the mountain, we set off down the drainage, finding icy, but manageable snow alongside the river. Though before long, the snowpack would recede to reveal the worst deadfall I have ever seen anywhere. Nothing can describe the pain of navigating this forest of fallen trees. At certain points, it would take 15 minutes to navigate 200 feet forward. At a certain point, despair started to hit the group. We were only a mile or two from the highway and our car, yet it seemed so improbable we would make it out before dark that we all went silent and moved forward with a grim acceptance that this would just be one of those walks where you suffer. Fortunately, we made our way back to the forest service road after climbing over what seemed like a million trees, and booked it back to the car. Getting my a-framed splitboard off my back after that was the most relief I may have ever felt

We stopped to get some Mud Man in Columbia Falls, which, I may add, is not as good as some people claim it is, before getting back to the cabin and knocking out. Looking back, it was a tough call; continuing to the next drainage over would have possibly exposed us to avalanche risk, though there was a snowmobile trail that would have delivered us to the car in a possibly quicker and definitely more enjoyable manner. Despite the heinous exit, I do want to visit this zone again, though I'm not sure if Henry and Elliot will be able to be convinced to go for round 2, even with a better exit plan.
