Moab, UT 3/18

Chris Jenemann 6/1/26

Ending a Great Trip

The concluding activity of our spring break excursion to the desert was a bike tour of the beautiful Arches National Park, via a rarely traveled route. After packing up our wonderful camp on the Colorado River, we drove out of the SR-128 canyon and set off from the Lion's Park. The group consisted of my buddies Elliot Stimpson, Jake Mathews, and me. We made our way out of Moab proper, out west to US-191, enjoying the wonderful bike paths past the Arches main entrance. Once off the bike path, we were faced with the first crux of the journey, the combination of a nonexistent shoulder and the souped-up ego that comes with a rented Jeep made our stint on the highway puckering. After laying down some watts to get off the busy road, we pulled off into Utah Raptor State Park.

After being let through the entrance gate free of charge(Thank you, nice lady), we headed out to the series of back roads that would lead us to Arches. This route enters the park through a back entrance that is significantly less traveled than the conventional routes to the park. We realized the implications of our more adventurous route when we hit the sand. When planning, we did not necessarily account for the poor combination of skinny tires and sand, and we suffered for our mistake. Regardless, we were treated to beautiful views of the La Sal Mountains.

We slogged through the sand until we reached the Arches entrance before slogging on deeper into the park. At points, when the sand got particularly deep or a hill was particularly steep, it became faster to push rather than ride. Even with the slow-moving, the ride was never unpleasant, and the awe of our surrounding kept group morale high.

A while later, we reached pavement and the main loop of the park, stopping shortly at Balanced Rock to take down some snacks before hopping back in the saddle. Taking advantage of the fast surface, we ripped through the main sections of the park. The combination of cathedral-like towers, smooth roads, and lack of cars on our side of the road made for some of the most fun biking I've ever had.

We moved quickly through the downhill before reaching a short but punchy climb, before the descent to the main entrances. I was promptly dropped due to a combination of my companion's superior fitness and my fiddling with my camera. I attacked the switchbacks down to the entrance, almost yelling with the excitement of hitting hairpin turns at 30mph in one of the most beautiful parts of our country. I caught up with my buddies, and we made the short ride to town before getting a quick diner lunch and booking it back to Helena.
