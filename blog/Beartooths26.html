Backpack

Bearthooths

East Rosebud, MT 8/17/25

Chris Jenemann 6/9/26

Before heading off to university, my dad and I headed east to one of our favorite areas, the Beartooths. We made the four-hour drive from Helena to the small collection of mountain cabins known as Alpine, Montana. We set off to hike a section of the Beaten Path, a trail that runs through the Absaroka-Beartooth Wilderness, connecting East Rosebud Trailhead in Montana to Cooke City, Wyoming. The trail is characterized by its endless alpine lakes, meadows, and granite peaks.

We were not going the whole way, but rather about a third of the way of the trail, where we would set up a base camp and then explore and fish the surrounding area. Doing the whole trail requires a lengthy shuttle, something we did not have the time or energy to set up. I look forward to coming back and doing the whole trail at some point. We made our way the 8 or so miles to Rainbow Lake, where we would set up camp for the next two nights.

The hiking was not too hard, and the scenery was beautiful. We had spent some time at the East Rosebud campground a few years earlier, so we were somewhat familiar with the area, though as we made our way farther from the trailhead, the backdrop became increasingly scenic. The fishing at Rainbow Lake was just ok though I was able to pull out a decent rainbow trout. We set up camp in a meadow with a few other groups, though by the end of the trip, we would have the place mostly to ourselves.

The next morning, we would pack our lunches and head farther up the trail to Big Park Lake in search of some better fishing. On the hike up, we found a bramble of huckleberries, which provided us with a delicious mid-morning snack on our way out of camp.

We found decent success catching small cutthroat in the shallow, muddy lake.

After some time at Big Park Lake, we backtracked to Lake of the Falls, a larger lake, looking for some better fishing and a spot to cook some lunch. Needless to say, the fishing improved, with both of us hooking up on some big cutthroats. This afternoon was really special.

On that small outcropping of rocks where the alpine granite gave way to the deep blue water of the lake, it really felt like the world gave way to the moment. Every few casts, you were on a fish, and when you weren't fighting, you were taking in the beauty of the surroundings and laughing with joy.

We hung around the lake for a few hours catching a few more fish and cooking out lunches on our camping stove. Honestly looking back there wasn't a bad moment on this trip. Everything from the weather to the fishing was great. Everytime I go out to the Beartooths I seem to have a great time!

The colors on these Cutthroats were crazy. They ranged from blue, almost purple on some, to bright blood red on others.

We headed back to our camp later in the afternoon. My dad decided to take a quick nap, and I headed off around the lake to check out some of the tributaries.

I spent the next few hours bushwacking around some small streams that fed into Rainbow Lake. Fishing the deep pools below boulders and rapids, I was able to catch quite a few of these small but stunning trout.

I'm pretty sure these were also Cutthroat, though I could be mistaken. Either way, I had a blast.

I made it back to camp and enjoyed the sunset with my dad. I think looking back, we both knew this would be one of the last times we got to spend real quality time together before the rush of college move-in hit. Looking back, I don’t think I could have asked for a better time. Spending time deep in the mountains with a loved one creates memories that seem clearer than others, the type that you carry for the rest of your life.

The next morning, I woke up early to catch a few more fish while my dad slept in. This might have been the most beautiful fish I have ever caught; the picture really doesn't do it justice.

Our hike out was pleasant and uneventful. We took our time meandering down the trail, stopping to check out some of the pools and cliffs carved by the stream.

Leaving the mountains and feeling the oppressive summer heat of eastern Montana wash over us, it felt like we were abandoning Wonderland. We ran into a lady from Eastern Europe, with whom we had a nice chat, before driving out of East Rosebud in search of the Candybars and Bathrooms of the Columbus Town Pump.
