Backcountry Ski

Copper Bowls

Lincoln, MT 4/26

Chris Jenemann 5/19/26

All time spring day

It was early April before the rush of finals when my buddy Fox Rowland invited Ollie Peine, Elliot Stimpson, and me to his families cabin in Lincoln, Montana. Conveniently enough, the cabin was only a few miles from Copper Bowls, a well-known Helena backcountry spot, so we decided to bake a quick tour into the weekend. In the morning, we woke up from our comfy shared beds in the attic of the cabin and took off in Fox's dad's Tacoma. We made the drive up Copper Creek Road, finding a clear road before hitting some snow whoops, which the Tacoma made quick work of. We parked about 1.5 miles from the bridge that marks the transition from fire road to proper skin track. We saw an old avalanche above the bridge on a small roller. A good reminder to remain cautious, though we would find a relatively strong snowpack.

We made quick work of the skin up to one of the bowls at about 8000 feet. I'm sure a hardcore Helena skier knows the name of our line, but I would just consult the GPS data below if you want specifics. Ascending the ridge up to our drop-in, we viewed another old avalanche, confirming our plan to take the time to dig a pit and discuss our options with the group. Although we had not seen any apparent sign of instability in the snowpack on the skin up, we remained cautious. The weather was great that day, and we had the drainage to ourselves.

We dug a pit at about 8000 feet on an east/northeast aspect and found a very stable snow pack. We could see where there was a refreeze crust that had formed, but it seemed to have healed under the new snow that had fallen since. The snowpack was deeper than our 240cm probes. We chose a line to lookers right of our pit on the open bowl with a moderate slope and long run out. I got the first drop, and it was wonderful. The snow was heavy, creamy, and a blast! I did a 500ish foot run down to the bottom of the bowl and waited for my companion to get their own piece of the cream cheese slope.

After regrouping at the bottom, Fox and Ollie decided to take a quick lunch while Elliot and I snagged a quick lap. Elliot cranked the music, and we made quick work of the skin up, putting down the 500-foot climb in under 15 minutes before cruising down the short but dreamy run. Meeting up with the guys again, we ate the last of our snacks before heading out of the bowl before the sun took its toll on the snow.

The ski out was a fun affair, cruising through the open forest of dead pines down to the fire road. As we left, the snow began to wetten rapidly, confirming our choice to head back to the cabin.

We hopped on the road and rode what was almost a board-cross style track of whoops left behind by the snowmobilers who frequent the area. After a fun leg burning descent we drove back to the cabin and enjoyed the post-climate disaster spring afternoon next to the Landers Fork, reading my copy of Alpinist magazine, as no one else had thought to bring reading material to a cabin with no internet.
