First Semester

Missoula, MT 2026

Chris Jenemann 6/8/26

This fall, I started my undergrad education at the University of Montana in Missoula. I was lucky enough to get a good scholarship, and I had a lot of friends already in or moving to Missoula, so it seemed like the right choice. Missoula is a great town, and living there makes it super easy to get outside. One of the first things I did was climb Sweeny Peak right outside of town with some high school and new college buddies.

It was baking that day, so walking up and down the ridge was hard, but we were rewarded with some cool, hazy views of the Bitterroot. Once back in town, we spent the afternoon by the river, something that would become an everyday occurrence during the heat of August and September. The first part of the semester was honestly a blur. I found myself lost in the freedom of college and didn’t spend much time reflecting or reaching out to new friends. School was easy, and the days were long, so I spent much of the time running, exploring the city, and enjoying the sun with my buddies from Helena.

Once fall kicked in, we headed across the border into Idaho to enjoy the Jerry Johnson Hot Springs, a pretty standard activity for Griz students.

The springs were warm, and the surrounding area was pristine. We didn't even see any nudists. At this point in the year, I was definitely starting to make more friends. I started to become especially close with Fox Rowland, someone whom I knew casually from high school cross country and work in our hometown, though we went to different schools. We became quick friends in college and would share some sweet adventures later on.

We camped along a river, not too far from the springs, before returning to campus in the morning. The stars were great that night.

I returned home to Helena to celebrate my birthday with my family and my birthday twin, Garrett Hinderman. We did a short hike in the Big Belts, where we saw some especially cute fall caves.

We also got some cool snowy views of the surrounding mountains from the top of Mount Edith. At this point in the semester, I was actually feeling less at home at college. I was homesick even being relatively close to home. School was not necessarily challenging, and I felt pretty directionless. Looking back, I think a lot of people were feeling similar things, and it’s just something you have to learn to deal with. Everyone tells you the transition away from the rigid structure of high school is hard, though it was especially hard on me. I thrived in the routine of distance running and hard academics, so when I came to college, and much of the struggle of racing and study was left behind, I lost direction for a bit. Things would start looking up as I made new friends, but it was hard to shake that feeling.

Towards the end of the semester, I would get out and do some climbing around Missoula, first at the Alberton area, then at Lolo Pass. Climbing helped me connect with some new people, most notably Olie Peine and Eli West, both of whom are great guys and I really enjoy spending time with.

Still, even when it seemed I was making new friends, I still felt very off. I’m not sure if it was a feeling of falling behind or stagnation, but I felt as though I wasn’t going through deep enough change. I imagined college as such a pivotal point in my life, and yet here I was not learning, not making that many new friends, and not really achieving anything at all. Was I just a bum? I ended up just running more and not really doing much, just exaggerating the problem.

I don’t want it to seem like I was depressed; I, for the most part, was still having a pretty good time. Life was low stress, and I was pretty consistently doing fun things. A big positive takeaway was learning to embrace myself more. t Being kinda nerdy and traditionally unathletic made me self-conscious for a lot of my life. Going to university made me realize that people really don’t care about anything besides your ability to just generally be a good dude. It's a lot harder to mess up socialy then i thought, not to say I didn't commit a few blunders here and there.

School was too easy for the first semester. My adviser made me retake Comp Sci 101 for no apparent reason, something that was a grave mistake. I knew how to write a for loop, and I know “hello world,” so I really gained almost no skill programming-wise through the University. Looking back, I'm not sure why I did not start coding stuff on my own, but I guess that just didn’t occur to me for whatever reason. I also took Calc two, which was a breeze, some honors courses, which were pretty okay, and Astronomy 101, which was the most engaging of the courses. Mark Reiser is genuinely one of the best educators I've had, and it was refreshing to go to a class that felt both educational and alive. I honestly should have taken harder courses this semester. I genuinely think the semester would have gone better if I had a non-zero course load.

Overall, my first semester of college was just ok. The biggest thing that characterized it was the feeling of being lost and expectation. I kept thinking something big was going to change, or a challenge would present itself and lead to some sort of growth, but that didn’t really happen, and why would it? Some great memories came with this part of the year, as the ones showcased above, the smaller ones, sitting in the sauna with my friends, or jumping off the bridge into the Clark Fork. All these things play into the experience. I don’t think there is a binary of good or bad first semester; rather, it just was. I can’t really put into words what I was feeling, but I will be interested to see what I think when I reread this later. Will I have those moments of immense change, will I let the nostalgia flow over these grey feelings and only remember the fun stuff, or will I look for that pivotal moment in my whole life? I don’t know, but we will see. Jesus, this was a rant. The last picture was from some insane northern lights after climbing at Lolo.
